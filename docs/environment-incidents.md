# Development environment — measured incidents

The dated record behind `docs/environment.md`: each freeze, kill and reset on this machine, its signature, and what fixed it. A `§7, <date>` reference in that file names the entry here; any other `§` reference is to that file.

- **Single runaway (30 Jul 2026) — the real failure mode.** One `claude.exe` grew 3826 → 7317 MB in ~60s; total hit 7841/7924 MiB. Recurred within the hour. The heap bound did **not** apply.
- **Concurrent sessions (31 Jul 2026) — normal operation.** 5–6 sessions at 250–500 MB, baseline never below 4 GB, some resident 3+ hours.
- **Interop-bridge wedge (1 Aug 2026) — not a memory fault.** Every `claude` launch hung with no output; memory was fine. The managed-policy queries (§9) stopped returning and accumulated, all state `S` at 0:00 CPU — nothing in `D` state, so not a storage stall. **Root cause unknown.**
- **Interop vsock stall (7 Sep 2026).** ~13:20 (mantahostels#3306); the probe could not tell it from the wedge. Two `/pick-next` dispatch waves were refused minutes apart, and both times the bridge tested healthy again shortly after with no `wsl --shutdown`. Timing six consecutive probes caught the mechanism: five returned in **0.03–0.04s**, one took **10.06s** and printed `WSL (3656361 - ) ERROR: UtilAcceptVsock:273: accept4 failed 110` — `ETIMEDOUT` on the interop vsock accept. **That day the stall was per-call and about 10s, and the next call was normal.** The refusals clustered on session spawns (load average 3.19–7.01), not at rest.
- **Degraded interop bridge (29 Oct 2026) — not load.** 10:20–11:40 (#3564). Every call returned, slowly: the policy query took **3.9–7.4s**. It held with every session idle and with none running, and only `wsl --shutdown` cleared it; afterwards the query took 0.03–0.07s. **Root cause unknown.** Ruled out: fragmentation (order-7+ blocks free in both zones), PSI memory and I/O pressure (near 0), the hvsock buffer (28672, as §1 sets it), the agent-listing storm (#3561), and a vmbus channel leak (one sample).
- **Interop vsock stall, held (3 Nov 2026).** ~16:05–16:21 (#4102). New tabs stayed blank and queries kept taking **about 10.06s**, with `accept4 failed 110` in `dmesg`, until `wsl --shutdown`; afterwards the query took 0.03–0.06s. **Root cause unknown.** Ruled out: a failed ring allocation (no `page allocation failure` that boot) and the hvsock buffer (28672, as §1 sets it). The lead is WSL 3.0.3.0: microsoft/WSL#42029 reports the same shape on that build and is open.
- **Host suspend (27 Aug 2026) — a freeze no recorder here can catch.** Windows entered Modern Standby on its 60-minute mains idle timeout at 17:20:53 and the WSL2 VM froze for 22 minutes: the guest journal is silent 17:25:36 → 17:47:52, `earlyoom` missed its 17:39 report and `wsl-pro` its ~17:45 restart. **Memory was not the cause and never close to it** — `earlyoom` read 6765 of 9924 MiB available at 16:39 and `~/memwatch.log` has no block after 12:11. On resume `claude agents` crashed and a new session would not start, their daemon sockets under `/tmp/cc-daemon-1000/` having died in the freeze. **Fixed by `powercfg /change standby-timeout-ac 0`**, so mains sleep is now never; **battery is still 25 minutes and a closed lid still suspends**, which is the residual. This is an ARM64 machine, so Modern Standby is the only sleep state available — `powercfg /a` reports S1, S2 and S3 all unavailable.
- **CPU saturation (4 Oct 2026).** Between 20:43 and 21:35 WSL froze repeatedly and the founder restarted Windows at 21:34:58 (Event 1074). `~/memwatch.log` read **load 112, 143 and 171** at 21:06, 21:29 and 21:35, at 705–781% of 800% busy, almost all of it `nice` — Claude sessions and what they spawn — with only **3.3–4.2 GB used**. At 21:28–21:29 systemd's 3-minute watchdog SIGABRTed journald, resolved, udevd and timesyncd, the kernel reported an RCU stall, and the interop vsock timed out (`accept4 failed 110`), which is what froze the terminals. Ruled out: memory (`earlyoom` read 7,059 MiB available at 21:18 and killed nothing) and host suspend (no Kernel-Power 506/507 between 19:51 and the restart). In the window: a full suite at 20:43 (605 s), a 51-file run at 21:05 that took 260 s, and a run at 21:24 the stall detector killed at ~21:35. **What filled the run queue went unrecorded** — memwatch then listed the top six processes by RSS only. New work now holds at the load ceiling (§6) (#830).
- **Battery standby, resumed broken (7 Oct 2026) — the 27 Aug residual, firing.** Unplugged, Windows entered Modern Standby on the 25-minute battery idle timeout at 09:08, deepened it at 12:24 ("Austerity Battery Drain Budget Exceeded") and woke on the lid at 22:39:30 (Kernel-Power 506/507). Unlike 27 Aug the VM did not simply pause and resume: from 22:43 the kernel reported RCU stalls (`timer wakeup didn't happen … Possible timer handling issue on cpu=0`), at 22:52 a Claude process sat stuck in-kernel in `link_path_walk` on CPU 1, the interop bridge wedged (§9), and `~/memwatch.log` read **load 27 at 60% busy** at 22:54 — tasks queued, not working. The guest journal stops at 23:02:53; Hyper-V removed the VM's NIC at 23:17:24. **Not the workload** — three Claude sessions, 2,370 MB combined, and `earlyoom` read 2,636 MiB available at 23:02 and killed nothing. **Why a resume breaks CPU 0's timer is unknown.** An RCU stall now warns at session start and refuses a dispatch — `scripts/check-kernel-stall.js` (#867). Battery standby stays on: never-sleep on battery was declined (#867). **Remedy after any long battery sleep: `sync`, then `wsl --shutdown`, before carrying on.**
- **New windows black, then closed (23 Oct 2026) — fragmentation, not exhaustion.** From ~09:50 a new `wsl.exe` tab showed only a cursor for about a minute, then closed; open shells kept working. Signature: `dmesg` repeating `page allocation failure: order:7` in `vmbus_alloc_ring ← vmbus_open ← hvs_probe`, and `/proc/buddyinfo` with **no free block of order 7 or above in either zone** while `MemAvailable` reads gigabytes. A finished-session reap frees memory but no order-7 block. **Fixed by the socket-buffer file in §1** (#2640).
- **Scheduler clock frozen (23 Oct 2026).** From 17:52 +08 every run queue's clock stood still while `/proc/stat` showed the CPUs busy: memwatch's one-second samples read 0.0 %CPU for every task. arm64 has no upstream fix (#2708). RCU stalls followed from 21:18, 3 h 26 min later, and the boot ran 2 h 10 min past the first until shut down by hand — unlike the 4 and 7 Oct stalls, which ended the VM within ~20 minutes (#2681). **A frozen clock now warns at session start and refuses a dispatch** — `scripts/check-kernel-stall.js` (#2779).
  **The clock's source is cgroup run time (#4092).** WSL's 6.18.54 kernel has no `CONFIG_SCHEDSTATS`, so no `/proc/schedstat`; both detectors sum `usage_usec` over `/sys/fs/cgroup/*/cpu.stat` against `/proc/stat`'s busy time, reading the children because a true root's figure is built from ticks and could never show a freeze. **No frozen sample exists on this source.**
- **Orphaned guard subshells (25 Oct 2026) — hooks timed out machine-wide for eleven hours.** From 04:20 +08 a scratch harness in `/tmp` ran a guard under `spawnSync("bash", [guard], { timeout: 8000 })`, whose timeout kills only the `bash` it spawned. Ten of the guard's forked subshells, still named `bash …/scripts/guard-cpu-load.sh`, were orphaned and kept spinning at 5–100% CPU each until the founder ran `wsl --shutdown` at 15:25. Kernel time rose from ~300% of 800% to 700–765% by 10:00, at load 20–100 with 2–3 GB used, and every session's guards hit their 10 s ceiling and blocked. No RCU stall (`check-kernel-stall.js --boot -1`); the boot ended in a clean poweroff. The boot before logged 73 `page allocation failure: order:6` lines in `vmbus_alloc_ring ← hvs_probe`, the 23 Oct fragmentation signature, which was secondary. **An orphaned hook run is now killed at session start** — `scripts/reap-orphan-hooks.js` (§8, #2922).
- **Fork chain at the pids cap (28 Oct 2026) — WSL's init killed.** From 15:21 +08 a run of `scripts/mutation-hold-gate.test.ts` started a loop between a test's git fake and `scripts/lib/host-git-guard/git`, each taking the other as the real git, and each pipeline hop forked a child while its parent waited. Processes rose from ~100 to 11,670 by 15:25 (`~/memwatch-minute.jsonl`) against `/init.scope`'s `pids.max` of 11,944, where every Claude session runs. The kernel refused forks there from 15:22:13 (`fork rejected by pids controller`), ~20 Claude processes aborted, and at 15:25:09 `/init` itself aborted; recovery was `wsl --shutdown`. **The guard now resolves git only from `PATH` after its own entry** (#3400).
- **Process runaway at 750 processes (30 Oct 2026) — the fleet stopped, no alarm.** From 03:07 +08 a scratch script, `/tmp/feed3583-g3-scripts/ap.sh`, whose shell function `seq` called itself, took processes from ~80 to 901 in 8 minutes, then held ~750 for 17 hours, every link one argv, with available memory near 1,200 MB against 5,400 before. Below the 2,000 process-count line nothing named the cause, and the kill tickets the refill loop did file (#3747, #3749, #3750) went unread. Now killed and ticketed at 10 minutes (§4, #3807). The kernel's OOM kill of the user manager at 20:23 is #3963; telling the founder from off the machine is #3964.
- **User manager OOM-killed (30 Oct 2026) — 45.6 hours down with 8.3 GB free.** At 20:23:21 +08, in the runaway #3807 reports, the kernel's global OOM killer took the 3 MB `systemd --user` (pid 419, `oom_score_adj` 100). `user@1000.service` failed at 20:25:09 and nothing restarted it until the boot on 1 Nov 18:11, while earlyoom read 8,336 MiB available from 20:29. `~/memwatch-minute.jsonl` has no line and the loop's log none from that evening until then, and no commit landed. **The fix is the manager's restart install** (§4, #3963).
- **Refill loop killed by earlyoom (3 Nov 2026) — 14 minutes down, ended only by a restart.** At 15:17:43 +08 earlyoom sent SIGTERM to the loop, pid 24322, `MainThread`, badness 668, 34 MiB, 7th in its cascade once larger processes had gone. The loop's last log line is 07:17:40 UTC, then nothing until `boot resume` at 07:31:38, after a distro restart. **The fix is the watch timer** (§4, #4139).
- **Windows watchdog reset, then the sign-in screen (3 Nov 2026) — 7.9 hours down, under a minute of it the crash.** At 01:52:53 +08 Windows hung in Modern Standby and the hardware watchdog reset it (Kernel-Power 41 and 142, bugcheck 0, no dump), with 3,660 MiB available, no earlyoom kill and no RCU stall. Why it hung is unknown. The VM starts only after sign-in, which came at 09:48:18; the loop resumed at 09:48:49 and took all 8 killed sessions back or finished them within minutes, with `git fsck` clean. **A sign-in after an unclean reset still waits for someone**; an update restart is covered by §9's keep-alive and ARSO (#4057).
- **Clock leap by host uptime, then a hung shutdown (3 Nov 2026).** After the founder's `wsl --shutdown`, the new VM started at 12:19:37 +08. At 12:21:48 the kernel reported `rcu_preempt self-detected stall`, `t=9435698 jiffies`, on a VM 131 s old. At `CONFIG_HZ=250` that is 37,743 s, against 37,734 s of Windows uptime. From 12:22:40 `load1` read 0.00 with 18-51 tasks running, `MemAvailable` stayed 2,372-2,913 MB and earlyoom killed nothing. A later `wsl --shutdown` hung, by the founder's report, and they restarted Windows at 12:37:54. `git fsck --connectivity-only` was clean and no worktree file read all-NUL, though scratch in `.claude/tmp` written from 11:50 did. **The reading: the VM's clock leaps forward by about the host's uptime**, at 90% (80-97%); mechanism and trigger are unproven, and load is not the trigger alone, since boot -2 reached load 33.48 at start without one (#4081).

  | 22 Sep 16:20 | 3.8 min | 635,983 | 646,619 | 1.6% |

  **All 3 stalls on a VM under 10 minutes old fit it; the other stalling boots were 1.4-8 days old.** Six other boots hold a `t`. Boots of 2, 15 and 29 Oct open at about 60 s. Three hold large figures that fit neither VM age nor Windows uptime, unexplained: 394,682 s on 22 Sep (VM 354,668 s, Windows 644,974 s), 41,966 s stamped 28 Sep 00:23:30, after that boot's last `--list-boots` entry, so its wall clock is suspect, and 159,032 s on 7 Oct (VM 263,080 s, Windows 263,775 s).

  **Why memwatch's clock line stayed silent at 12:22 cannot be determined.** The detector read a frozen clock at 12:22:44 over 1 s; memwatch wrote no `sched clock` line on that boot, and its passes' raw readings were not kept. Its line did fire over its 20 s passes in the freeze below, so the longer window alone is not the cause. The minute line's `sched` now keeps the readings, so the next freeze settles it.
- **Second freeze the same day (3 Nov 2026) — not the leap.** On the VM of 12:39, after two Modern Standby episodes at 15:05, memwatch logged `sched clock: frozen` at 15:12:44, 15:13:26 and 15:14:12; from 15:14:12 the run queues advanced 0.0 s on every pass while the CPUs read 4-29 busy CPU-s. `sched`'s kept readings show `/proc/stat`'s total ticks stepping too: +40,211 s in one 21 s pass, back 2,549 s, and once wrapping below zero. RCU stalls followed at 15:15:14 and 15:15:24 (`timer wakeup didn't happen`, `Possible timer handling issue on cpu=3`); the journal ends at 15:18:34 and `wsl --shutdown` worked. Its largest `t`, 2,728 s, is near neither VM age (9,375 s) nor Windows uptime (9,419 s), so it reads as the 7 and 23 Oct shape. Standby as its trigger is weak, 35% (15-55%). Both freezes were on WSL 2.7.11; 3.0.3 went in at about 15:40 (#4084), and #4093 (the recurrence watch) counts from there (#4081).

- **Windows watchdog reset, awake and under load (4 Nov 2026).** At about 12:08 +08 Windows hung and the hardware watchdog reset it (Kernel-Power 41 and 142, bugcheck 0, no dump, `ConnectedStandbyInProgress=false`, lid open). The guest journal stops mid-test at 12:07:23 with no shutdown. memwatch's last block, 12:07:47, read 636% of 800% busy, 247% of it sys, load 15.6, 10 Claude sessions and 6,871 MB used. earlyoom killed nothing and the kernel logged no RCU stall. No session ran a shutdown or reboot. **Both watchdog resets came under sustained load**: mean busy over the prior hour was 672% (3 Nov 01:52, not idle as its standby state suggests) and 553% here. **Load is not sufficient**: 28 Oct held 609 minutes at 600% or more, one run 136 minutes long, with no reset. A "System Hardware Update (200.0.21.0)" was pending and uninstalled. Cause unknown. **Remedy tried: that update, moved the firmware from 9.166.235 to 9.225.235.**

- **Freeze under a touched test run, with Windows out of memory (6 Nov 2026).** At 13:12 +08 a 137-file `npm run test -- --changed 2ee984a93` froze the VM for about 3 minutes: memwatch logged nothing 13:13:19 → 13:16:35, four tests hit ~145 s timeouts and one worker stopped answering; no other session ran. The guest was never short: `MemAvailable` 8,025 → 6,896 MB, PSI memory 0, swap flat at 384 MB, no earlyoom kill, no RCU stall. **Windows ran out and paged**: `win_avail_mb` fell 2,748 → 1,944 MB while Windows read 171,984 pages in a minute. The 4 Nov reset fits the same reading, Windows free 651–895 MB for its last 8 minutes with paging spikes to 168,477 pages a minute. Two more freezes followed, 13:22–13:24 with WSL idle (load1 0.77) and Windows free 3,626–3,890 MB, Kernel-Processor-Power 37 logging firmware CPU limiting for 109 s after it, and ~13:27–13:33, ended by `wsl --shutdown`; these two each began within a minute of a session's `powershell.exe` call, where the 13:12 one followed none, only memwatch's own read after every line. **Leads, none settled**: the interop bridge wedging under Windows calls (§9), firmware CPU limiting, Windows paging.

- **Third watchdog reset, in Modern Standby, on the updated firmware (9 Nov 2026).** Kernel-Power 41 and 142 at 03:25:43 +08, bugcheck 0, no dump, `ConnectedStandbyInProgress=true`, lid closed, on AC; no 1074 restart request. The guest journal runs normally to 03:24:24 and stops. memwatch read 613–740% busy with 5–9 sessions from 02:22 to 03:10, then 146–250% from 03:21; 187% busy, 3 sessions and Windows free 799 MB at 03:24:11. Windows free memory read 499–1,248 MB all hour. **Over 4–9 Nov, 41 hours ran heavy (mean ≥550% busy) and 38 of them with Windows free under 900 MB, so the two conditions cannot be told apart in the log; both measured resets fell in those hours, none in the 47 light ones.** Cause unknown; the root-cause search is the map at #4264.

- **A Windows Update restart, not a crash (12 Nov 2026), and 7¾ hours lost to it.** `MoUsoCoreWorker.exe` asked a planned restart at 01:29:09 +08 (User32 1074, "Service pack (Planned)"), 29 minutes after active hours (07:00–01:00) ended, and `TrustedInstaller.exe` two more at 01:32 and 01:33; every boot logged a clean last shutdown and no Kernel-Power 41. Nobody signed in until 09:14:08 (Winlogon 7001), so the VM, which starts only after a sign-in, stayed down and memwatch logged nothing 01:28 → 09:14. Automatic Restart Sign-On (ARSO) was on, by the founder's check, and still signed nobody in at any of the three boots: the account was set to Windows Hello only (`PasswordLess\Device` `DevicePasswordLessBuildVersion` 2), and ARSO saves a password hash, which a Hello-only sign-in never gives it. With that setting off and one password sign-in, a `shutdown /g` restart logged LSA/Operational event 320, signed in 16 s after boot and locked 6 s later, and the keep-alive started WSL 10 s before the founder's first unlock (#5042). That VM then died at 09:17:51 with no Windows or Hyper-V event, two minutes after interop logged `UtilAcceptVsock accept4 failed 110` (§9), C: at 99% (7.7 GB free); cause unknown. **Remedy: the §9 watchdog task now boots the VM whenever it is down, and ARSO signs in after an update restart now Hello-only is off (#5042).**
