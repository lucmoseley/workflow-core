A COMMIT CHANGING A LINE ON A DECLARED SENSITIVE PATH IS HELD WHILE A PLANTED BUG ON THAT LINE SURVIVES EVERY TEST. StrykerJS plants the bugs, its mutants, on the lines the commit adds or changes there, and the hold refuses while it cannot judge them too: tests red on HEAD plus the commit's changes, Stryker or vitest not installed, or Stryker not finishing — fix what the refusal names and commit again. Where it lists mutants, `Survived` means every test still passed with that one in place, `NoCoverage` that no test ran the line at all. Clear each one of two ways. Write a test that fails with that mutant in place — its line names the file, line, column, mutator and what the source was changed to, so no run by hand is needed. Or, where the mutant cannot change behaviour (an unreachable guard, a sort tie-break, display text), put `// Stryker disable next-line <mutator>: <why it cannot change behaviour>` above its line, or wrap a message table in `// Stryker disable <mutators>: <why>` … `// Stryker restore <mutators>`. A disable comment with no reason after its colon is refused, naming its line, where this commit adds it, turns old text into it, or widens its reach over a line it adds. Mutants on lines this commit did not change are never charged to it, and there is no score to meet (#1851).
