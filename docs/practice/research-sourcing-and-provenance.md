# research-sourcing-and-provenance

Researched: 2026-10-24

## Ranking sources of evidence

- Grade a body of evidence, not one study. A single study is weak evidence; a systematic review or meta-analysis ranks above it, and a recognised body's guideline at or above both. Cochrane Handbook ch. 14 with GRADE ("a body of evidence from randomized trials begins with a high-certainty rating", lowered for "risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias"; cochrane.org); GRADE Working Group ("More than 120 organizations from over 20 countries … have endorsed or are using GRADE", gradeworkinggroup.org) [standard or field body]; Wikipedia, "Identifying reliable sources (medicine)" ("Using small-scale, single studies makes for weak evidence"); McGill Library, 6S pyramid guide (guidelines one level above reviews, "Single studies is the bottom level") [named expert]
- A standards body's document carries consensus only at its consensus status: check the status, not the publisher. IETF RFC 2026 §4.2.2, §5 (a Best Current Practice has "the technical approval of the IETF"; an Informational RFC "does not represent an Internet community consensus or recommendation") [standard or field body]
- Expert opinion is not evidence: weigh the reasons and observations an expert gives, not the conclusion alone. Schünemann, Zhang, Oxman, "Distinguishing opinion from evidence in guidelines", BMJ 2019 ("expert opinion is not the same as evidence"); Wikipedia, "Reliable sources" (a self-published expert counts only when "an established expert on the subject matter") [named expert]; GRADE blog, "Expert evidence framework" (2021) [standard or field body]
- A vendor's or project's own docs are a primary source about that product only: good for plain facts about it, weak for an evaluation or a general claim, since the author has a vested interest. Wikipedia, "Reliable sources" §Self-published sources (usable "about themselves", not for "claims about third parties") and "Independent sources" ("can be used to fill in noncontroversial details or to provide the subject's viewpoint"); Garousi, Felderer, Mäntylä, grey-literature guidelines, IST 106 (2019), Table 7 ("Is there vested interest? E.g., a tool comparison by authors that are working for particular tool vendor"); Wineburg and McGrew, Stanford working paper 2017.A1 (fact-checkers judge a site by what other sites say of it) [named expert]
- Popularity is not evidence of quality. Wikipedia, "Reliable sources" (user-generated sites "are generally unacceptable as sources"); Wineburg and McGrew 2017 [named expert]
- Use the highest tier that exists, and say so where you drop a tier. A field body may use a practitioner survey for what data cannot yet show. McGill 6S guide ("begin your search at the highest possible level") [named expert]; OWASP Top 10 2021 introduction (8 of 10 categories "from contributed data", 2 from a survey, since "contributed data is looking into the past") [standard or field body]

## Applying it to practice notes

- This repo's four tiers fold GRADE's and 6S's levels: a field body's guidance on top; a paper, review or expert verdict with reasons next; surveys and curated lists below; a source about itself cited for a general claim, or popularity, unverified. No source states this mapping, so it sets no default [unverified]
