# code-review-process

Researched: 2026-10-22

## What review is for, and what it finds

- Defects are the main reason people give for review, but they are a small share of what review finds. At Microsoft, finding defects was the first reason for 383 of 873 programmers (44%). But only 78 of 570 review comments (14%) were about defects: "the fourth most frequent, out of nine". Code improvements were the largest group (165, 29%). Of the 78 defect comments, 65 were logic issues, 6 were high-level and 5 were security. Bacchelli, Bird, "Expectations, Outcomes, and Challenges of Modern Code Review", ICSE 2013, §IV.A, §V.A (zenodo.org/records/1401198) [standard or field body]
- Defect comments "mostly address 'micro' level and superficial concerns"; reviewers "focus on formatting mistakes because they are easy". Understanding comments were the second-largest group: "context and change understanding is the key of any review". Bacchelli, Bird 2013 §V.B, §I [standard or field body]
- Microsoft (Czerwonka et al.): "Only about 15% of comments provided by reviewers indicate a possible defect, much less a blocking defect". Maintainability comments are "at least 50% of all". A reviewer new to the code gives about 33% useful comments; by the third review of the same code, this rises to about 67%. Czerwonka, Greiler, Tilford, "Code Reviews Do Not Find Bugs", ICSE-SEIP 2015 §II (microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/PID3556473.pdf) [standard or field body]
- Open source projects show the same split: about 75:25 between maintainability and functional problems, and 7-35% of review comments are discarded. Beller, Bacchelli, Zaidman, Juergens, "Modern Code Reviews in Open-Source Projects: Which Problems Do They Fix?", MSR 2014, abstract (the full text at repository.tudelft.nl could not be reached; unread) [standard or field body]
- At Google, people expect four things from review: "education, maintaining norms, gatekeeping, and accident prevention". Review is also used later for "tracking history". "Defect finding is welcomed but not the only focus." Sadowski, Söderberg, Church, Sipko, Bacchelli, "Modern Code Review: A Case Study at Google", ICSE-SEIP 2018 §4.2 (sback.it/publications/icse2018seip.pdf) [standard or field body]
- Google's standard for approval: "favor approving a CL once it is in a state where it definitely improves the overall code health ... even if the CL isn't perfect"; "there is no such thing as 'perfect' code—there is only better code". google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/standard.html [named expert]
- Unread: IEEE 1028-2008, Standard for Software Reviews and Audits (paywalled), and Mäntylä, Lassenius, "What Types of Defects Are Really Discovered in Code Reviews?", IEEE TSE 2009 (paywalled; other papers cite its evolvability share as about 75%). [unverified]

## Change size, and a large change in parts or as a whole

- Google: "100 lines is usually a reasonable size for a CL, and 1000 lines is usually too large". Also "A 200-line change in one file might be okay, but spread across 50 files it would usually be too large". Reviewers may reject a change "for the sole reason of it being too large". Split a change by stacking, by files, horizontally (by layer) or vertically (by feature). google.github.io/eng-practices/review/developer/small-cls.html [named expert]
- At Google the median change is 24 lines, over 35% of changes touch one file, and about 90% touch fewer than 10 files. Comments per change peak at 12.5 for changes of about 1250 lines. Sadowski et al. 2018 §5, Finding 4 [standard or field body]
- At Microsoft the share of useful comments falls as the number of files grows. The fall "only starts to be noticeable for reviews with 20 or more changed files". Czerwonka et al. 2015 §II [standard or field body]
- SmartBear's Cisco study: "review no more than 200 to 400 lines of code (LOC) at a time". There is "a significant drop in defect density at rates faster than 500 LOC per hour", and "Do not review for more than 60 minutes at a time". A review of 200-400 LOC over 60-90 minutes "should yield 70-90% defect discovery". This is the vendor's summary of its own study. The original Cohen article (stickyminds.com) returned 403, and the book "Best Kept Secrets of Peer Code Review" was not read. smartbear.com/learn/code-review/best-practices-for-peer-code-review/ [named expert]
- When a large change cannot be split, review its design first. Google: "at least write some comments on the overall design of the CL and send it back". For any change, take "a broad view" first ("Does this change even make sense?"), then the main files, and send major design comments "immediately", before you read the rest. google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/speed.html §Large CLs; .../navigate.html [named expert]
- A diff alone does not show enough. "Sometimes you have to look at the whole file to be sure that the change actually makes sense"; also look at "the CL in the context of the system as a whole". google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/looking-for.html §Context [named expert]
- LLM reviewers do worse on large changes too: "As the number of issues in a PR increases, ACR tools experience a sharp drop in Recall while Precision remains stable". Zeng et al., "SWR-Bench", arXiv 2509.01494 v2 (2026), a preprint with no venue [named expert]
- Thus a large change needs either a split or a design pass first, and a review of every line must not be a review of only the hunks. Derived from the rows above [unverified]

## Checklists and separate review axes

- Give each reviewer a distinct focus; this beats one generic checklist for everyone. In three studies with 60 professional developers at Bosch Telecom, reviewing code documents, "PBR is more effective than CBR (i.e., it resulted in inspection teams detecting more unique defects than CBR), and ... the cost of defect detection using PBR is significantly lower". Laitenberger, El Emam, Harbich, NRC ERB-1059 (1999) / IEEE TSE 27(5) 2001, abstract [standard or field body]
- The gain does not come from less overlap between reviewers. The same study found that "defect overlap has nothing to do with the impact of the use of PBR on inspection effectiveness". The gain comes from each perspective finding more of its own class of defects. The perspectives must together cover the whole artefact (Basili: "a combination of techniques in the family provides coverage of the entire document"). Laitenberger et al. §9 [standard or field body]
- Checklists help most with omissions: "Checklists are the most effective way to eliminate frequently made errors and to combat the challenges of omission finding". smartbear.com/learn/code-review/best-practices-for-peer-code-review/ [named expert]
- A checklist alone does little for novice reviewers. In an experiment with mostly novice reviewers (ad hoc, checklist, guided checklist), the authors found no strong relationship between the guidance given and review performance. Gonçalves, Fregnan, Baum, Schneider, Bacchelli, EMSE 27(4):99, 2022 (abstract only; the Springer full text was not reached, unread) [standard or field body]
- Google names the axes a reviewer checks: Design, Functionality, Complexity, Tests, Naming, Comments, Style, Consistency, Documentation, Every Line, Context, Good Things. "look at every line of code that you have been assigned to review." google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/looking-for.html [named expert]
- Machines should catch the mechanical checks. Google shows static analysis in review (Tricorder, 110 analyzers). Sadowski et al. 2018 §5 [standard or field body]
- Anthropic's `code-review` plugin keeps conformance apart from bug finding. It runs "2x CLAUDE.md compliance agents: Redundancy for guideline checks", a bug detector "Focused on obvious bugs in changes only", and a git-history agent. For a CLAUDE.md finding, the scorer "verifies guideline explicitly mentions it". github.com/anthropics/claude-code plugins/code-review/README.md, one vendor's product cited as an adopter [unverified]

## Dispositions and follow-up

- Label the severity of each comment. Google: "Nit: This is a minor thing", "Optional (or Consider)", "FYI: I don't expect you to do this in this CL". Without labels, "authors may interpret all comments as mandatory". google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/comments.html §Label comment severity [named expert]
- Conventional Comments: `<label> [decorations]: <subject>`. The labels are praise, nitpick ("should be non-blocking by nature"), suggestion, issue, todo, question, thought, chore and note ("always non-blocking"); the decorations are `(blocking)`, `(non-blocking)` and `(if-minor)`. GitLab requires this format: "Use the Conventional Comment format to convey intent". conventionalcomments.org; docs.gitlab.com/development/code_review/ [survey or curated list]
- Fix it now, not later. Google: "unless the developer does the clean up immediately after the present CL, it never happens". Also "If a CL introduces new complexity, it must be cleaned up before submission unless it is an emergency". google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/pushback.html §Cleaning It Up Later [named expert]
- Defer to a ticket only for problems that the change exposes but did not cause. Google: "If the CL exposes surrounding problems and they can't be addressed right now, the developer should file a bug for the cleanup and assign it to themselves", with an optional TODO that references the bug. GitLab: "If a comment can be addressed in a follow-up issue, work with the maintainer on a path forward", and a TODO must link its issue. Same page; docs.gitlab.com/development/code_review/ [named expert]
- Decline a finding when the author is right. Google: authors "are closer to the code than you are"; if their argument holds, "let them know that they are right and let the issue drop". google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/pushback.html §Who is right? [named expert]
- You may approve while comments are still open, if the reviewer trusts the author to address them, or the comments are optional, or they are minor. "The reviewer should specify which of these options they intend". GitLab: "For non-blocking suggestions, consider approving the MR before passing it back". google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/speed.html §LGTM With Comments; docs.gitlab.com/development/code_review/ [named expert]
- In Google's tool, an unresolved comment is an action item to "definitely address", and a resolved comment can be optional or informational. GitLab: "Only resolve threads you have fully addressed". Sadowski et al. 2018 §5 step 3 [standard or field body]; docs.gitlab.com/development/code_review/ [named expert]
- Anthropic tags each finding "Important, Nit, or Pre-existing, so a bug the PR didn't introduce doesn't look like the author's fault". It sorts each comment by the author's response: "they fixed it, said it was wrong, said the behavior was intentional, or decided it didn't matter". It also counts reports on known-clean PRs that "would have led someone to make a harmful change". It flags "the class of bug, so the author fixes every instance, not one line". Osmani, Westlee Lunt, Wang, "How the Claude Code team does code review", 2026-10-15, claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/ [named expert]
- Explanations given only in the review are lost. Google: "Explanations written only in the code review tool are not helpful to future code readers"; usually the code should be rewritten more clearly. google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/comments.html §Accepting Explanations [named expert]

## Re-review after fixes

- Tools support a review of only what changed since the last review. Gerrit: first-time reviewers "look at its diff against its base"; on a later visit they "may see what has changed since that version by comparing the old patch against the current patch". gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/user-review-ui.html [standard or field body]
- Bitbucket's iterative review is for "removing the need to re-review code you've already seen". It shows "just the files changed in the commit(s) since you last reviewed the PR". It is not available after "a merge commit or destructive force push since your last review". atlassian.com/blog/bitbucket/iterative-review, one vendor's product [unverified]
- Google's Critique lets the author and reviewers "look at diffs between any pairs of snapshots to see what changed". GitLab: "Push feedback-based changes as isolated commits". Sadowski et al. 2018 §5 step 4 [standard or field body]; docs.gitlab.com/development/code_review/ [named expert]
- A rebase between rounds adds changes that are not part of the fix. Gerrit "identifies parts of the diff that were modified due to rebase", shows these "rebase edits" in different colours, and skips a file whose edits are all from the rebase. gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/user-review-ui.html [standard or field body]
- An approval covers only the code that was reviewed. In Gerrit, an approval is copied to the next patch set only if its `copyCondition` matches (for example `changekind:TRIVIAL_REBASE` or `NO_CODE_CHANGE`). In GitHub, you can "dismiss stale pull request approvals when commits are pushed that affect the diff". Anthropic: "An approval covers one specific commit". gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/config-labels.html §copyCondition [standard or field body]; docs.github.com/.../about-protected-branches, one vendor's product [unverified]; claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/ [named expert]
- Repeated full passes do not converge. On one Anthropic PR the agent author and agent reviewer went through "more than fifty rounds". On git blame, "about 59% of it was code that had been there since the reviewer's first pass. The reviewer was rarely finding new bugs, it was finding a few more old ones each round." Anthropic's fixes: a cap of three rounds, then reconsider the approach; "Diff-only re-review after the first few passes"; "No repeats of findings already posted". The share of PRs that passed after one round rose from 65% to 78%. claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/ [named expert]
- Superpowers re-reviews a fix from "FIX_BASE", which "is the head the previous review saw". "New Critical/Important breakage in the fix diff joins the open findings list", and "New findings on untouched code go to the ledger, not the loop". There are at most five rounds per task, and "a silent discard is forbidden". github.com/obra/superpowers skills/subagent-driven-development/SKILL.md, references/fix-loop.md [survey or curated list]

## LLM and agent reviewers

- Precision is low. SWR-Bench has 1000 manually verified GitHub PRs, and half of them are clean, so any comment on a clean PR is a false positive. The best system had an F1 "of only 19.38%". "A primary factor limiting higher F1 scores for all techniques is their low precision". Four of the five techniques had "precision scores below 10%". Zeng et al., arXiv 2509.01494 v2 (2026-06), a preprint [named expert]
- The main causes of false positives, from 100 sampled: "Lack of Contextual Understanding (48%)", "Over-sensitivity to Modification (17%)", "Vague and Unactionable Feedback (16%)" and "Lack of Domain Knowledge (13%)". If you combine several independent review reports, F1 rises by up to 43.67% and recall by up to 118.83%. Same preprint [named expert]
- LLMs judge correct code against a natural-language spec too harshly. They "frequently misclassify correct code implementation as non-compliant or defective", and "more detailed prompt design, particularly with those requiring explanations and proposed corrections ... leads to higher misjudgment rates". The fix is a "Fix-guided Verification Filter": run the original code and the model's proposed fix against tests, and use the result as "executable counterfactual evidence". Jin, Chen, arXiv 2508.12358 (ASE 2025 NIER) [standard or field body]; arXiv 2603.00539 (2026), a preprint [named expert]
- In industry, authors resolve many of the comments, but there is a cost. At Beko (Qodo PR-Agent; 4,335 PRs, of which 1,568 had automated review): "73.8% of automated comments were resolved", but the mean time to close a PR rose from 5 h 52 min to 8 h 20 min. The drawbacks were "faulty reviews, unnecessary corrections, and irrelevant comments". Cihan et al., "Automated Code Review In Practice", ICSE-SEIP 2025 (arXiv 2412.18531) [standard or field body]
- At Atlassian over one year, 38.70% of RovoDev comments led to a code change, PR cycle time fell by 30.8%, and human-written comments fell by 35.6%. The authors work at the vendor. Tantithamthavorn et al., ICSE-SEIP 2026 (arXiv 2601.01129) [standard or field body]
- Verify each finding before you post it. Anthropic: "Agents hunt for bugs in parallel, verifiers vote on each finding, survivors ranked by severity". A finding is posted only "if enough of them agree it's real". Filters for style-only and cleanup-only findings were "A/B tested ... on our own PRs before keeping them". The share of internal PRs with substantive comments rose from 16% to 54%. claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/ [named expert]
- An agent reviewer does not replace the human. Osmani: "Use AI as first-pass reviewer, not final arbiter"; "No matter how much AI contributed, a human must take responsibility". Böckeler used a review agent "to double check AI's work against the original prompts". It caught excess code, "though we've seen the reviewer delete too much code in its attempt to fix that". Anthropic: automated review can clear a low-risk change, "but it can't fill a slot that calls for a human". addyosmani.com/blog/code-review-ai/; martinfowler.com/articles/pushing-ai-autonomy.html (2025-08-05); claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/ [named expert]
- Thus an agent reviewer needs a verify or arbiter step for each finding (a vote, a confidence score, or a test of the proposed fix), a prompt that does not invite fault-finding on spec conformance, and a human or ruling session that owns the final call. Derived from the rows above [unverified]

## Tools

- Claude-compatible, from the vendor: the Anthropic `code-review` plugin, also run locally as `/code-review`. It runs four parallel agents (2 CLAUDE.md compliance, 1 bug, 1 history) and one confidence scorer per issue on a 0-100 scale, then applies "Filter out any issues with a score less than 80". It drops pre-existing issues, "Pedantic nitpicks" and "Issues linters will catch". github.com/anthropics/claude-code/blob/main/plugins/code-review/README.md [standard or field body]
- Also from Anthropic: `/security-review`, the security review GitHub Action and the Claude Code GitHub Action. `CLAUDE.md` and `REVIEW.md` guide the reviewer. The hosted "Code Review for Claude Code" is a paid Team and Enterprise feature. claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/ [named expert]
- Superpowers (Jesse Vincent; MIT) has `subagent-driven-development` with a scoped re-review fix loop and a findings ledger. Simon Willison vouches for the plugin as a whole, not for this skill (simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/10/superpowers/). github.com/obra/superpowers [survey or curated list]; 303,311 stars on 2026-10-22 (check only)
- Qodo PR-Agent (open source) is the tool that Cihan et al. evaluated in industry. Cihan et al. ICSE-SEIP 2025 [standard or field body]
- No MCP server for code review was found that a standard body or a named expert vouches for. Search of 2026-10-22 [unverified]
